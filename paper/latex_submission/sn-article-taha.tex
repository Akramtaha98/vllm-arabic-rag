\abstract{Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems deployed for Arabic applications face a memory bottleneck: Arabic's morphological richness fragments subword-tokenized text into more tokens than equivalent English text, inflating the Key-Value (KV) cache vLLM-class engines must maintain per request. We introduce a Lightweight Semantic Pruning Middleware (LSPM), a thin layer between retrieval and generation that scores passages at the sentence level with a cross-encoder and forwards only the highest-relevance subset under a fixed or load-aware ratio. We report four preliminary findings. First, across 30 hand-verified Arabic-English sentence pairs, two tokenizers confirm an aggregate Arabic-to-English token ratio of 1.76x and 1.69x (bootstrap $p<0.0001$ versus parity). Second, a fidelity pilot sweeping three compression ratios ($r=0.3, 0.5, 0.7$) shows pruned answers remain consistent with unpruned ones (mean ROUGE-L 0.89--0.93, BERTScore-F1 0.96--0.97). Third, re-testing the naive-truncation baseline on 140 real ARCD questions (980 live generations) for adequate power, LSPM significantly beats naive truncation head-to-head on token-F1 at the most aggressive ratio ($r=0.3$: +0.114 F1, Holm-adjusted $p=0.005$) and is equivalent to the unpruned answer at $r=0.3$ and $r=0.7$ (TOST, $p=0.038$, $p=0.003$), while naive truncation is significantly worse than unpruned at $r=0.3$/$0.5$ (adjusted $p=0.0004$, $0.015$); at $r=0.5$/$0.7$ the head-to-head advantage is positive but non-significant. Fourth, absent GPU access, we give an analytical KV-cache-savings projection from Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct's architecture and the measured context reduction (9.7--19.1 MiB/request), not a throughput claim. We release the implementation, raw data, and a live demo.}
